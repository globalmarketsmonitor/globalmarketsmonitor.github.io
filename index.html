    How the numbers are built
    • Money in is AMFI’s “funds mobilised”: purchases, SIP instalments, switch-ins and new fund offer money. Money out is redemptions and repurchases, including switch-outs. Net inflow = money in − money out.
    • SIPs: the monthly total, SIP accounts (new, discontinued or matured, at month-end, and those that paid an instalment) and SIP assets come from AMFI’s SIP page, which is updated with its monthly data. A month is used only if its total is stated at least twice on the page and every statement agrees (the month-wise table, the accounts table, the page text), every financial-year total equals the sum of its months, and the figure is within ±25% of the month before (a guard against misreads). Account figures are used only if they add up to the year-to-date row and roll forward month to month (previous + new − discontinued = month-end). Stoppage ratio = SIPs discontinued or matured ÷ new SIPs. The split by category comes from SEBI’s monthly “Status of Mutual Fund Industry” table (from August 2026), several weeks later; its totals include domestic fund of funds and are compared with AMFI’s for every month both cover (see Sources & checks). SEBI’s category amounts are shown only when they add up to its own total; account figures that don’t add up are shown as published and flagged.
    • Lumpsum & other = money in − SIP. It includes switch-ins, transfer plans and new fund offers, so it is not purely fresh lumpsum money.
    • Categories: AMFI’s reports use SEBI’s revised scheme categories from August 2026 (for example value and contra, and sectoral and thematic, are now reported separately, index funds and ETFs are split by asset class, and life cycle funds are new). Long-run charts join old and new categories so each series continues.
    • Checks: an AMFI month is used only if money in − money out = net for every category and the categories add up to every sub-total, section total and the grand total. It is then compared with SEBI’s copy of the same table (assets, folios and net flows by category). NSDL’s monthly foreign-investor figures are reconciled with NSDL’s financial-year table, and fund-house totals with AMFI’s monthly reports.
    • Timing: AMFI’s report and SIP figures come out around the 10th of the following month (checked every hour from the 7th to the 16th, and every few hours otherwise); SEBI’s table follows weeks later; NSDL updates daily; SEBI’s fund trading data daily with a lag of a few days; fund-house data each quarter; direct-plan data monthly.
    • Folios are accounts, not unique investors. Average assets (fund houses, direct plans) are monthly or quarterly averages; other asset figures are month-end.
    • For information only; not investment advice.

    How the view is built
    • Scores run from −1 to +1: ▲▲ strong overweight (≥0.45), ▲ overweight (≥0.15), ● neutral, ▼ underweight, ▼▼ strong underweight. Confidence is High when most signals agree and Low when they conflict or data is thin; thin evidence is capped at one notch.
    • Beyond-equity assets, tactical: valuation 30% (real price vs 10-year history; income assets vs their 10-year trend; TIPS by real yield; credit by spreads), carry 20% (futures roll yield or yield over cash), trend 20% (12-month momentum and price vs 40-week average), macro fit 20% (exposure to today’s growth, inflation, liquidity, risk and dollar regime), positioning 10% (CFTC, contrarian at extremes).
    • Strategic: expected 5-year real return from building blocks — equities: dividend yield + IMF trend growth − dilution (0.5% US, 1% other developed, 1.5% emerging) + half the gap back to normal valuation + real currency drift; bonds: yield − expected inflation (− credit losses); commodities: cash + roll yield (20% today’s, 80% a long-run −1%) + real-price drift; gold: 0.5% + real-price drift. Diversification counts 30% for beyond-equity assets.

    Methodology & sources
    • Regime lights: each pillar averages simple, published rules (shown under each light), each scoring +1, 0 or −1. Above +0.33 is green, below −0.33 red. Rules use 3-month changes or levels against 10-year history so one noisy week does not flip the regime.
    • Percentiles and z-scores use 10 years of weekly data (daily for Treasury yields, monthly for CPI and OECD indicators).
    • Live Indian PE: NSE India's live index feed (PE, P/B, dividend yield on consolidated trailing earnings), read every 10 minutes in market hours on this public edition; every figure carries NSE's own time stamp. History: daily NSE valuation series via indexpe.in, with 7/10/15-year and full-history medians and percentiles.
    • Markets: Yahoo Finance (indices, ETFs, FX, commodities, VIX, MOVE). US yields: US Treasury daily nominal and TIPS real par curves; breakevens and the 5y5y forward are computed from them. CPI and policy rates: Bank for International Settlements. Fed balance sheet: Federal Reserve H.4.1. Leading indicators: OECD via DBnomics. FII/DII flows: NSE.
    • Credit spreads, financial conditions and GDPNow come from FRED. Add a free FRED API key as the repository secret FRED_API_KEY to switch them on. Until then, credit risk appetite uses the HYG/IEF ratio.
    • Global valuations: ETF trailing PE (Yahoo) as a live proxy; Siblis Research month-end PE and Dec-21–Dec-25 year-ends for history. ERP = earnings yield − real 10-year yield (US: TIPS; India: 10-year G-sec − CPI).
    • Updated by hand: Siblis month-end figures and the central bank calendar (lib/config.mjs, lib/events.mjs). The India 10-year yield is now live, with a hand-entered fallback in lib/macro-config.mjs. Dates are shown wherever these are used.
    • For information only; not investment advice.
